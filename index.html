Who makes probe cards

A small industry with outsized leverage: every advanced chip made anywhere crosses one of these companies' products before it is packaged.     Technology and market coverage

    What moves the market

    • HBM and AI accelerators. Both need full-wafer or near-full-wafer cards with tens of thousands of tips, and both refresh every year. FormFactor's record 2025, Technoprobe's 2026 guidance and MJC's +42% forecast all cite the same driver.
    • Pitch. Micro-bumps under 40 µm make cantilever cards physically impossible and push value to MEMS and vertical technologies, where a handful of companies hold the process know-how.
    • Geography. Demand sits in Taiwan, Korea, Japan and the US; export controls have cut Chinese revenue for the majors and opened room for domestic Chinese suppliers on mature nodes.
    • Lead time. A probe card is built for one design, and a fab cannot ramp a product until it arrives. Whoever turns a new design into a qualified card fastest wins the socket. The next page is about that.

    The robotics case

    Here is the argument. Probe cards are getting denser (tens of thousands of springs at pitches a human hand cannot work) and design cadence is getting faster (a new AI accelerator every year, chiplets every quarter). Yet much of the industry still builds cards with skilled hands and with pick-and-place cells that were engineered for one product family. Every new design means new fixtures, new motion code and weeks of requalification. Flexible robotics changes the unit of work from "build a cell for this card" to "load a recipe for this card". The company that makes that switch first ships new designs sooner, wins the socket, and compounds the advantage. Play with the models below and see whether you agree.

    What "modern robotics" actually means here

    • Precision arms and cobots with vision and force control. Small six-axis arms place springs within microns; cobots and mobile robots move magazines and substrates so people never carry parts.
    • One software platform for every cell. Behaviour-tree orchestration, a simulated digital twin for bring-up, fleet management and observability, and CI/CD for the cell code, so a new cell is configured, not written.
    • Recipes generated from the design. The card's own design file becomes the placement program. No fixture drawings, no hand-taught points.
    • AI inspection in the loop. Anomaly-detection models on every tip and every probe mark, feeding back into the process instead of a rework queue.
    • Learned policies for the messy parts. Vision-language-action models for handling variation: a bent spring, a new tray, a tip geometry that did not exist last quarter.

    This page argues a position. The numbers in the models are illustrative, chosen to show the shape of the trade-off rather than any company's actual data. Disagree in the comments; that is the point.
